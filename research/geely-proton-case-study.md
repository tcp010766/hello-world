# Case Study: Geely × Proton (吉利 × 宝腾), 2017–2026

**How a Chinese automaker turned around Malaysia's national car company, and what it shows about moving supply chains from China to ASEAN**

**As of:** October 2026. This is a companion to `china-to-malaysia-supply-chain-shift.md`. Sources are at the end. Figures marked † come from widely reported public record and were not re-verified in this research pass.

---

## 1. Summary

In 2016 Proton was a loss-making national champion:
- About 72,000 units produced, down 30% year on year.
- Domestic share around 12%, down from more than 70% in the 1980s–90s.
- About RM1bn in annual losses.
- A RM1.5bn government soft loan, conditional on finding a foreign partner.

In 2017 Geely bought **49.9% of Proton** (and 51% of Lotus) from DRB-HICOM. Instead of injecting large amounts of capital, Geely transferred **platforms, management systems, purchasing scale and an export network**. DRB-HICOM kept 50.1% and the "national car" identity.

**Results:**

| | 2016 | 2025 | 9M 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales (units) | ~72,000 | 157,976 | 160,886 (already above all of 2025) |
| Domestic share | ~12% | 19.4% | ~26–27% YTD |
| Exports | negligible | ~6,000 (highest since 2012) | new monthly highs |

- Proton leads Malaysia's EV segment with the e.MAS 5 and e.MAS 7. The company claims more than 60% EV share.
- The **Automotive High-Tech Valley (AHTV)** is planned to make Tanjung Malim Geely's right-hand-drive (RHD) production and export hub.
- Target for 2026: 200,000 units. Target for 2029: become Malaysia's #1 brand, ahead of Perodua.

**Core insight:** this was not a factory relocation. It was a **capability transplant into a local brand that already had political legitimacy, a dealer network and an installed plant**. That is why it worked where many stand-alone Chinese entries in ASEAN struggle.

## 2. Before the deal: why Proton was failing (1985–2016)

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ageing, licensed technology | Founded in 1983 under Mahathir's industrial policy, using Mitsubishi technology. After Mitsubishi left, Proton tried to develop platforms in-house (Campro engine, Gen.2, Persona, Preve). It lacked the scale to fund competitive R&D. |
| Shrinking protection | Its advantage depended on tariff and excise protection. As AFTA and Japanese local assembly expanded and **Perodua** (Daihatsu/Toyota-backed) took the entry segment, Proton's share fell from more than 70% to about 12–15%. |
| Quality reputation | Long-running complaints about build quality, electrical faults and resale value. |
| Weak supplier base | Many small local vendors, low volume per part, high piece cost, and inconsistent quality. |
| Exports never scaled | Repeated attempts in the UK, Australia, the Middle East and China failed. |
| Lotus drain | Lotus, bought in 1996, needed continuing capital. |
| Fiscal burden | Government support included the RM1.5bn soft loan in 2016, conditional on finding a strategic foreign partner. |

**Sales decline:** 2014: 115,783 units → 2015: 102,175 → 2016: about 72,290 (production).

**Partner search (2016–17).** PSA (Peugeot-Citroën), Renault, Suzuki and Geely were considered. Geely withdrew one bid for a controlling stake in March 2017, then returned in May with a **minority offer**. Malaysia preferred that, because it preserved national ownership.

---

## 3. The deal structure (May–June 2017)

| Element | Terms |
|---|---|
| Equity | Zhejiang Geely Holding takes **49.9% of Proton Holdings**. DRB-HICOM keeps **50.1%** (control and national brand). |
| Cash | About **RM170.3m** of Geely equity investment. |
| Technology in kind | The **Boyue SUV platform** (became the Proton X70) was transferred, valued at about **RM290m**. Later platforms were licensed: Binyue/Coolray → X50, Haoyue → X90, Emgrand → S70, Galaxy E5/Xingyuan → e.MAS 7, and others. |
| Clean-up | Non-automotive assets worth about RM1.2bn moved from Proton to DRB-HICOM and were pledged to the government. Proton repaid RM567m owed to DRB-HICOM. |
| Lotus | Geely bought **51% of Lotus**. Etika Automotive (also controlled by Syed Mokhtar Al-Bukhary) took 49%. Lotus became Geely's global luxury and EV brand (Lotus Technology listed in 2024 †). |

**Why this structure was clever:**
- **Low cash, high capability.** Geely paid relatively little cash. Most of the value it brought was intellectual property (platforms) and know-how, which cost Geely little at the margin because the engineering was already paid for by its Chinese volumes.
- **Political risk handled through minority ownership.** Malaysian control defused nationalist objections. Mahathir criticised the sale in 2018, and MITI publicly defended it.
- **Balance-sheet clean-up first.** Separating non-core assets and debt before Geely entered gave the operating company a clean starting point.

## 4. The turnaround playbook: what Geely actually changed

### 4.1 Product: Chinese platforms with Malaysian adaptation

| Model | Launch | Geely base | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| X70 | Dec 2018 (CBU), CKD Tanjung Malim from 2020 † | Boyue | Proved "Chinese platform + Proton badge" could win. First Proton SUV. |
| X50 | Oct 2020 † | Binyue / Coolray (Volvo-derived 1.5T) | Volume B-SUV, a Japanese-quality alternative at Proton prices |
| X90 | 2023 † | Haoyue | 7-seat mild hybrid |
| S70 | 2023 † | Emgrand | Sedan to replace the Persona/Preve segment |
| e.MAS 7 | Late 2024 (CBU) → Tanjung Malim CKD (Sept 2025) | Galaxy E5 / Xingyuan family | #1 EV in Malaysia for five straight months in 2025 |
| e.MAS 5 | 2026 | Geely EX2 / Xingyuan | RM57k–70k entry EV; 9,356 units in Jan–May 2026 |
| Saga, Persona, Iriz | (kept and refreshed) | Proton in-house | Kept as the domestic "people's car" volume base |

**Pattern:** Proton kept its own low-cost models (the Saga is still a top seller) and **added Chinese platforms in every segment where Proton could not afford development**: SUV, hybrid, EV.

Adaptation for Malaysia included:
- Right-hand drive.
- Tropical calibration.
- Malay-language voice control ("Hai Proton").
- Local styling and badging.
- Pricing below Japanese competitors.

### 4.2 Manufacturing and quality

- Geely-style **manufacturing systems and quality audits** were brought into the Tanjung Malim plant, with investment in the paint shop and SUV CKD lines †.
- **Chinese executives in key operating roles:** CEO Dr Li Chunrong (2018–2021) †, plus heads of manufacturing, quality and purchasing. Malaysians kept board, government-relations and sales leadership. Leadership passed to a Malaysian CEO in 2021 †.
- **Phased CKD:** CBU import first (to test the market) → CKD assembly → rising local content. This let Proton launch products quickly while the supply base was being built.

### 4.3 Supply chain and purchasing (the less visible lever)

- **Vendor rationalisation and development.** Fewer, larger suppliers per part. Geely supplier quality engineers worked with local Proton vendors. Chinese tier-1s were invited to localise for Geely-platform parts.
- **Purchasing scale.** Proton parts could be co-sourced with Geely's global volumes (over 4 million units a year for Geely Holding in 2025), which cut piece prices.
- **A hybrid supply model:**
  - **Local:** body-in-white, plastics, seats, glass, wiring.
  - **Imported from Geely's ecosystem:** powertrain components, electronics, EV battery packs and e-drive at first.
  - **Next phase:** Proton's Tanjung Malim engine plant can now build hybrid, PHEV and EV components, with a target of 400,000 units by 2028.

### 4.4 Distribution and brand

- Showrooms were refreshed and upgraded to "3S/4S" standard (sales, service and spares, plus customer surveys).
- A separate **e.MAS** EV brand was created, so EVs were not weighed down by Proton's older brand image.
- **Proton International Corporation** was set up in July 2025 to handle exports. In 2025 it sold about 6,000 units: Egypt 2,280 and Vietnam 1,316 (under the Geely brand). The target is tenfold growth by 2030.

### 4.5 Financial outcome

- DRB-HICOM returned to profit in FY2022, with pre-tax profit of RM410m against a RM291m loss in FY2021. Proton's volume was the main driver: 141,432 units in 2022, the highest since 2013.
- Proton's standalone profit history is **not fully public** in the sources reviewed, so "profitable since year X" claims should be checked against Proton/DRB-HICOM filings.

- EV penetration rose to about 9.2%, from 5.1% in 2025.
- **Proton, a Chinese-technology brand with a Malaysian badge, is gaining share faster than the Chinese brands themselves.**

## 6. AHTV: from turnaround to regional hub

- **Automotive High-Tech Valley, Tanjung Malim, Perak.** A DRB-HICOM–Geely development worth about **RM32bn** over roughly 10 years.
- **Ambition:** 500,000 vehicles a year by 2035, about half for export. Malaysia becomes **Geely's RHD hub** for ASEAN, the UK, Australia, Japan, South Africa and parts of the Middle East. (In 2023 Geely reportedly dropped Thai EV production plans to focus on Malaysia.)
- **What is in place:**
  - Proton's EV plant opened in September 2025: 20,000 units a year in phase 1, rising to 45,000.
  - Zeekr local assembly has been announced.
  - Proton's Shah Alam production is moving to Tanjung Malim.
  - Supplier parks are under way.
- **Neighbour and competitor:** BYD's own CKD plant on a separate 600,000 m² site in Tanjung Malim is due in 2H2026. Tanjung Malim is becoming a cluster of Chinese-technology auto plants.

## 7. Why it worked: success factors

1. **The right equity split.** 49.9/50.1 gave Geely enough influence to run operations and Malaysia enough control to keep it legitimate. The partners shared upside, and the local side owned the politics.
2. **Technology was the main contribution.** Already-developed platforms cost Geely little at the margin and were worth a great deal to Proton. Proton got roughly 10 years of R&D for about RM290m.
3. **An existing brand, plant and dealer network.** Geely avoided greenfield risk, distribution build-out and brand-building costs, which are the three things that usually defeat foreign entrants in ASEAN.
4. **Geely had done this before.** It had already integrated Volvo (2010), leaving Volvo's identity intact while sharing platforms (CMA, used in the Binyue/X50). Geely applied the same "**respect the brand, share the architecture**" model.
5. **Phased localisation.** CBU → CKD → local content let products launch fast while suppliers caught up, and the timing matched Malaysia's incentive deadlines.
6. **Segment gap-filling, not replacement.** Proton kept the Saga and other low-cost models and added Chinese platforms only where it could not compete alone.
7. **Clear operational authority.** Geely executives ran manufacturing, quality and purchasing in the early years, then handed over to Malaysian leadership once systems were in place.

## 8. Risks and criticisms

| Risk | Detail |
|---|---|
| Technology dependency | Proton's competitive product range is now almost entirely Geely-derived, and its own R&D capability has narrowed. Critics ask whether this is "a Malaysian badge on a Chinese car". If the relationship soured, Proton would have little to fall back on. |
| Low local value-add in EVs | Battery cells, e-motors and power electronics still come largely from China. The local-content story is stronger for ICE models than for EVs. |
| Cannibalisation | Geely now sells its own brands (Zeekr, Geely Galaxy, Lynk & Co †) in Malaysia alongside Proton. Proton has to manage overlap with its own shareholder's brands. |
| Domestic market ceiling | The total industry is about 815,000 units a year. With more than 10 Chinese brands competing, price wars and dealer stress are likely. AHTV's 500,000-unit ambition **only works if exports scale**, and 2025 exports were about 6,000. |
| Trade and geopolitics | Exporting Chinese-platform vehicles from Malaysia to the UK, Australia or the US may face origin and security scrutiny (connected-car rules, FEOC-type tests). Malaysia's 2025 trade agreement with the US commits it to align with US economic-security measures. |
| Margins | Leading on price (for example the e.MAS 5 at RM57k) builds share but may compress margins. Proton's standalone profitability is not transparent. |
| Politics | National-car politics persist, as Mahathir's 2018 criticism showed. A change in government sentiment could change the terms. |

## 9. Lessons learned

### For Chinese firms going abroad (出海)

1. **Buy into legitimacy instead of building it.** A minority stake in a trusted local brand can be worth more than a wholly owned greenfield plant.
2. **Lead with technology, not cash.** Platforms, systems and supplier access are cheap for the giver and valuable to the receiver, which is the best kind of deal.
3. **Leave the local identity intact.** As with Volvo, the brand, the people's-car models and local leadership were all kept, and the systems underneath were changed.
4. **Send operators early and hand over later.** Chinese managers in manufacturing, quality and purchasing at the start, then a visible handover to local management.
5. **Plan exports from day one.** The host-country market alone rarely justifies the investment. Malaysia's value to Geely is as an RHD export base, and that part is still mostly unproven.

### For host governments

6. **Tie support to a strategic-partner condition.** The 2016 soft loan's condition (find a foreign partner) forced the decision that saved Proton.
7. **Keep control and grant operating freedom.** A 50.1% hold kept the political settlement stable without blocking day-to-day change.
8. **Negotiate for capability, not just capital.** Next time, write in explicit commitments on R&D localisation, local engineering headcount and supplier development. This is where the Proton deal is weakest.
9. **Use incentive deadlines.** The end of EV CBU duty exemptions pushed localisation at Proton, BYD and others.

### For supply-chain practitioners

10. **"Made in Malaysia" ≠ "independent of China."** Proton's bill of materials, especially for EVs, still runs through Geely's Chinese supply base. Treat it as geographic diversification, not political diversification, and map tier-2 and tier-3 origin before relying on it for tariff or security purposes.
11. **Partner-owned brands are a supplier-risk category of their own.** If your OEM customer or supplier depends on one foreign technology partner, that partner relationship is a single point of failure. Monitor it the way you would monitor a sole-source part.

## 10. Scorecard

| Dimension | Score | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial turnaround | ★★★★★ | Sales up about 2.2× from 2016; share from about 12% to about 26% |
| Product competitiveness | ★★★★★ | Competitive in SUV, hybrid and EV segments |
| Quality and brand perception | ★★★★☆ | Much improved; legacy perception persists |
| Local technology capability | ★★☆☆☆ | Dependent on Geely platforms |
| Local supply-chain depth | ★★★☆☆ | Good for ICE models; EV supply still China-based |
| Export platform | ★★☆☆☆ | About 6,000 units; AHTV ambition unproven |
| Political sustainability | ★★★★☆ | The minority structure has held through changes of government |

**Overall:** one of the most successful Chinese outbound automotive investments, and a model for "capability transplant" deals. Whether it succeeds in the long run depends on exports and on deepening local technology, not on domestic sales.
